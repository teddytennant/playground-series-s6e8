WHAT I NOTICED, AND WHY IT CANNOT WAIT A RUN
--------------------------------------------
w46 section 2 asserted, in `w46c_predlb.py`'s module docstring and in the journal,
that the -29.82e-6 miss on the five 08-21 files "is NOT model form and it is NOT
drift", and closed off extrapolation with this check:

    "In-sample residual by CV quintile is FLAT (+1.24/+0.05/-1.07/+0.02/-0.22e-6),
     so the linear-in-CV slope is not failing at the top of the range and this is
     not an extrapolation artefact."

That check cannot detect what it was used to rule out.  w30b is ordinary least
squares with a constant and a CV term, so its residuals are ORTHOGONAL TO CV BY
CONSTRUCTION: sum(r) = 0 and sum(r*cv) = 0 are normal equations, not findings.  A
flat in-sample residual-vs-CV profile is guaranteed whatever the truth is.

And the design point matters here in a way w46 did not check.  The 78 fitted files
top out at cv 0.9701180.  The five 08-21 files run cv 0.9701220 to 0.9701400 -- four
of the five are STRICTLY ABOVE THE ENTIRE FITTED CV RANGE, and the fifth sits at its
edge.  Every one of the ten files queued for 08-22 is in that same region.  So the
one check w46 ran to dismiss extrapolation is mechanically uninformative, and the
five points it was defending are pure extrapolation.  That does not make w46c wrong;
it makes it UNTESTED where it claimed to be tested.

THE HYPOTHESES, REGISTERED WITH THRESHOLDS
------------------------------------------
E1  EXTRAPOLATION.  Refit w30b's exact form on the low-CV part of the 78 and predict
    the held-out top of the CV range.  If the top-band mean held-out residual is
    <= -10e-6 at a comparable dCV, then a material part of the -29.82e-6 is the
    predictor failing above its support, NOT an ad>=195 era effect.
    >= -3e-6 -> extrapolation is not the story and w46c's era reading survives.
    In between -> partial; report the split, do not round it to one story.

E2  GENERIC PROSPECTIVE OPTIMISM.  Expanding-window: for each day D from 08-13 to
    08-20, fit on every file first scored BEFORE D, predict day D.  Let m = the mean
    of those day-ahead residuals.  |m| < 5e-6 -> the predictor is prospectively
    unbiased on old design points, and the 08-21 miss is specific to something about
    those files.  m <= -10e-6 -> the predictor is generically optimistic out of
    sample and w46c's era term is partly re-labelling a known bias.

E3  THE CLAIMED PRECISION.  w46 section 2 scored the miss at z = -8.6 against
    resid_sd_new = 7.756e-6, which is an IN-SAMPLE dof-corrected residual sd on a
    sample containing near-twin files (w30b's own docstring records a test-set rho of
    0.999979 for one pair).  Registered: the leave-one-out held-out residual sd will
    exceed 7.756e-6.  If it exceeds 11e-6, then |z| for the five falls below 6 and
    the "not a draw" claim, while probably still true, was overstated as written.

E4  SLOPE vs LEVEL, and this is the one that changes tomorrow's reading.  w30b's CV
    slope is 1.856 e-6 of LB per e-6 of CV, fitted on 78 files that include near
    twins.  A slope fitted too steep over-predicts by (1.856 - s_true) * dCV above
    the fit centre, which is a RAMP in CV; an era effect is a LEVEL.  Registered
    discriminator, computable tomorrow on n=15 (the five already scored plus the ten
    queued, which span dCV ~ 18e-6):
        regress (actual - w30b prediction) on cv among the 15.
        slope <= -0.6 per e-6  -> the miss is at least partly a bad CV slope, and the
                                  correct fix is a re-estimated slope, NOT a dummy.
        slope within +/-0.4    -> a LEVEL shift, and w46c's dummy is the right form.
    Whichever way it reads, `ERA_SHIFT` gets re-estimated on all 15 points, which is
    w46 section 8 item 3.
