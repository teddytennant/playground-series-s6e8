§2. THE CV-SIDE QUESTION, WHICH IS *NOT* FORBIDDEN AND IS THE REAL TEST
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3 forbids moving on an LB measurement. It does not forbid asking whether the CV number
itself is honest. There is a named mechanism and it predicts §0 exactly:

  The member matrix is 187 OOF vectors produced on the frozen SKF5 seed42 folds. The
  combiner is cross-fitted on THOSE SAME folds. For a stacker fit on folds != k, the member
  values of its training rows were produced by member models that were trained on sets
  INCLUDING fold k. That is the standard same-folds stacking leak. It is mild — but a
  BETTER-CONVERGED combiner can exploit it MORE, because exploiting it is exactly what extra
  optimisation of a 187-way correlated design buys. Standardisation's whole documented effect
  (w23 §1) is that it lets lbfgs actually converge.

  If that is what is happening, the standardisation's cross-fitted CV gain of +8.46e-6 is
  partly leak, its honest value is smaller, and the LB residual shrinks toward noise.

There is already one data point and it points that way. w23c's holdout — an 80/20 row split,
combiner fit on the pool and scored on rows it never saw, so the stacker-weight overfit is
measured directly — gives:

    std1_tol1e-4 (standardised)                        hold_auc 0.9703699240
                                                        difference  +3.06e-6

+3.06e-6 on a holdout against +8.46e-6 cross-fitted. But w23c is n=1, on ONE transform

§5. WHAT WOULD MAKE ME WRONG ABOUT §2
--------------------------------------
The leak story predicts the gain shrinks on a holdout. A competing and duller story is that
the +8.46e-6 is real and the LB slice simply dislikes this file — in which case the holdout
reproduces the cross-fitted number and S1 fires. A third story: the holdout fits on 553k rows
where the cross-fit also fits on 553k rows, so if the leak is the mechanism the holdout is
NOT actually leak-free either — its members' OOF values were still produced with fold
knowledge. This is a genuine limitation and it means w25d can only detect the part of the
optimism that is stacker-weight overfit to specific ROWS, not the part that is member-level
fold leakage. If D comes back near +8.46e-6, that does NOT fully clear the leak hypothesis;
it only clears the row-overfit half. Recorded now so the result is not over-read later.

THE FINDING THAT NEEDS A LOCAL TEST
------------------------------------
Send 10 (`w23_ad187std_logit`, CV 0.9700298) printed 0.97114 against a registered fixed-offset
prediction of 0.97114 and a low-CV-artefact prediction of ~0.97105. It is +65e-6 of CV above
any logit file previously sent, so the confound between "logit family offset" and "the gap
shrinks as CV rises" is broken. The logit family gap is REAL: +151.1e-6 over h3 (t +14.6), and
reproduced out of sample to 2.7e-6.

+151e-6 is 18x w17a's entire simulated paired slice budget of 8.21e-6. It is not a slice draw.
The live hypothesis is a TRAIN -> TEST effect:

  H_L: the OOF member columns are produced by 5-fold models and the test member columns by
       full-data models, so the test columns are strictly better inputs. The logit transform
       is unbounded and is the most sensitive of the four to member quality, so it gains more
       from that upgrade than rank-based transforms do. Cross-fitted CV therefore
       SYSTEMATICALLY UNDERSTATES every logit-containing mix on the test set.

If H_L is true it is decision-relevant, because h3 EXCLUDES logit and ens4 INCLUDES it, both
`WANTED` files are h3, and the h3-vs-ens4 CV margin in the 187 pack is only +2.9e-6 against a
+14.0e-6 ens4 gap term. Nothing has been acted on, because H_L is estimated entirely from
public-LB data and that is exactly the input that produced the rogii-wellbore failure.

THE TEST — w26a, and it needs NO LEADERBOARD AT ALL
----------------------------------------------------
H_L makes a purely local, purely CV-side prediction: if better member columns are worth more to
logit than to the rank-based transforms, then WORSE member columns must cost logit more. So
degrade the member matrix by a controlled amount and measure the slope of stack AUC against
member quality, per transform.

  Take the 187-member OOF matrix O. For a grid of degradation levels d, form O_d by adding
  Gaussian noise at d x the per-column sd (d in 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40), fixed seed. For
  each d and each transform in (logit, hybrid, rankraw, rescale), cross-fit the combiner on the
  frozen SKF5 seed42 folds and record CV. Fit CV against d per transform; the coefficient is
  that transform's member-quality sensitivity S_k.

  A second, cleaner arm if compute allows: instead of noise, degrade by SUBSETTING members
  (187 -> 150 -> 120 -> 90, fixed random draws, 3 reps). Subsetting degrades quality without
  changing the noise model, so agreement between the two arms is the check that S_k is a
  property of the transform and not of Gaussian noise specifically.

PREDICTIONS, registered now:
  Q1  S_logit is the steepest of the four, and by a wide margin. If logit is NOT the most
      sensitive, H_L is FALSIFIED and the +151e-6 gap has no local mechanism.
  Q2  The ORDERING of S_k across the four transforms matches the ordering of the family
      offsets in w25f (logit +151.1 > rescale +37.8 > rankraw +15.1 > hybrid +6.1). This is
      the strong form and it is the one worth testing, because the offsets were fitted with no
      knowledge of S_k. A rank correlation of +1.0 across four transforms is p = 1/24 under a
      null of no relation — weak on its own, but it is a genuine prediction.
  Q3  The magnitude is checkable, roughly. The OOF-vs-test member quality gap is one 5-fold
      step, i.e. members trained on 80% vs 100% of the rows. If S_logit's slope implies a
      logit advantage of the right ORDER (tens of e-6, not tenths and not thousands) for that
      much quality change, H_L survives; if it implies 1e-6 or 5000e-6, it does not.

WHAT I EXPECT TO BE WRONG ABOUT
--------------------------------
The most likely failure is that ALL FOUR transforms turn out similarly sensitive, because the
combiner is a logistic regression on 187 highly-correlated columns and the ridge may wash out
transform-specific sensitivity entirely. That would leave +151e-6 with no mechanism at all,
which is a worse position than today's but an honest one. Registered so a null result reads as
a result rather than as a failed run.
