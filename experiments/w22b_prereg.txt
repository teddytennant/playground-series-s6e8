w22b — addendum prereg, written after w22a printed and BEFORE w22b was run.

w22a's results are in and R1 fired: joint P(all ten reverse) = 0.1900 against a mean
marginal of 0.2802, correlations 0.940-0.993, and the #reversed histogram is BIMODAL
(60.9% of slices reverse zero pairs, 19.0% reverse all ten, only 20% in between). The ten
pairs are one latent coin flip. w16s's defence is CONFIRMED as stated.

w22a's M5 also came back reassuring: corr(public delta, private delta) = -0.205 for every
pair, and P(h3 wins private | public reversed) is 0.879-0.976, HIGHER than the
unconditional 0.838-0.956. Conditioning on all ten reversing at once: 0.905-0.979.

WHY THIS ADDENDUM EXISTS. That -0.205 is a MIXTURE of two channels that point opposite
ways, and which one dominates decides whether 10/10 is a threat or not. w22a cannot
separate them because it re-draws the pseudo-test block every rep:

  BETWEEN-BLOCK: if the drawn 296,302-row block is simply a bad block for h3, BOTH of its
  halves are bad. This channel is POSITIVE and it IS the threatening one — it would mean
  the public reversal is evidence the whole test set disfavours h3, which would carry
  straight into private.

  WITHIN-BLOCK: for a FIXED block, the two halves partition it exactly, so a half that
  runs h3-poor forces its complement h3-rich. This channel is NEGATIVE and harmless — it
  is pure split noise and it anti-carries into private.

The observed -0.205 says the negative channel currently wins on net. But a net figure is
not a decomposition, and "the public reversal might mean the test rows disfavour h3" is
precisely the hypothesis the w21 addendum flagged as the live threat. It has to be
measured, not inferred from a mixture.

DESIGN: nested. B = 40 pseudo-test blocks of 296,302 rows; S = 50 independent public/
private splits within each block. 2000 evaluations, the same cost as w22a. Same seed
family, same f = 0.20, same pairs, same OOF vectors.

MEASUREMENTS:
  N1. WITHIN-block corr(public delta, private delta), pooled over blocks. Prediction: this
      should be close to -1, because within a fixed block the two halves are near-exact
      complements. If it is not near -1, my understanding of the design is wrong and
      nothing in N3 stands.
  N2. BETWEEN-block corr: correlate the block-mean public delta against the block-mean
      private delta across the 40 blocks. Prediction: strongly POSITIVE, near +1, since
      both halves estimate the same block-level quantity.
  N3. THE DECISION NUMBER, computed within block: P(h3 wins private | the public half
      reversed), pooled over blocks, and the per-block spread. If the within-block figure
      is ~0.95+ then the 10/10 reversal is split noise and the pick is safe. If the
      per-block SPREAD is wide and some blocks sit below 0.5, then block identity matters
      and the threat is real for an unlucky block.
  N4. Variance decomposition of the public delta into between-block and within-block
      components. This is the cleanest single statement of how much of the reversal risk
      is "unlucky test set" versus "unlucky split".

PRE-REGISTERED INTERPRETATION:
  S1. If the between-block variance share is SMALL (<= ~25%), the public reversal is
      mostly split noise, it anti-carries into private, and the deadline pick is safe on
      this axis. Close the question.
  S2. If the between-block share is LARGE (>= ~60%), then the public reversal is largely
      evidence about the test rows themselves, it carries into private, and w16s's
      defence — though correct about the 10/10 being one draw — is defending the wrong
      thing. That would be a real finding against both deadline picks.
  S3. My honest prior: between-block share MODEST, around 20-40%, because the public half
      is only 20% of the block so within-split noise on it is large. So I expect S1, but
      not overwhelmingly.
  S4. Reported whichever way it comes out. WANTED is not moved this slot regardless — no
      submission is possible — but if S2 fires it is written up as the top next-step and
      the deadline pick is flagged, not quietly left.
