2. THE INSTRUMENT (w66a_pricercal.py)
================================================================================
PART A -- THE OUT-OF-SAMPLE CALIBRATION SLOPE. n = the 19 scored stems that are absent from
`w52b_cvlb93.csv` and carry an OOF. For each, pred = `w53a_pricer.predict_lb(cv, stem)` with cv
recomputed from the OOF; z = (LB - pred)/1e-6. GLS of z on [1, (pred - mean pred)/1e-6] under
Omega = Sxx + R, where Sxx = St + Sp is built by `w63a_setprice.fit` (NOT re-implemented) and
R = I * (1e-5)^2/12 is the LB reporting-rounding term. The calibration slope is
beta_total = 1 + c, c the fitted slope. Intervals scaled by sqrt(max(chi2/dof, 1)), one-sided.

⚠ Omega is the covariance of (LB - CV), not of (LB - pred), and pred applies a slope >1 to CV,
so Omega UNDERSTATES the true dispersion. That is the conservative direction here -- chi2/dof
then exceeds 1 and the one-sided scaling widens every interval. A sensitivity arm refits with
Omega' = Sp + s^2 (Sxx - Sp) + R at s = BASE_SLOPE and the SIGN of every verdict must survive.

PART B -- THE FULL CV<->LB TABLE UNDER THE RIGHT COVARIANCE. All scored stems with an OOF and
a family in w53a's fitted design. GLS of z = (LB - CV)/1e-6 on w53a's OWN design row
[1, cv6, fam dummies, std, corr, era:cv6] under the same Omega, against OLS on the same design
with the same variance scale. The question is not "what are the coefficients" -- OLS already
answers that and is unbiased. It is whether the SES in `w30b_corrterm.json` mean anything, given
that all 93 LB values are one draw of ONE public slice and w64 measured those errors correlating
at ~0.9999. An se computed as if they were independent is a different number from the truth.
